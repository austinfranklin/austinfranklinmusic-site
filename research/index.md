<h3>A Live Coding SOM Application for Local Repositories</h3>
<p>This paper describes the engineering of The Unfinder, a standalone C++ application that combines granular synthesis, unsupervised self-organisation of sound archives, and a live-coding language called PLISP into a single real-time performance system. The application is organised around seven components: an offline analysis pipeline, a corpus representation, a self-organising map, a live-coding interpreter and text editor, a navigable map display, a concurrent decode cache, and a granular synthesis engine. It discusses the role of the playlist, PLISP's mutable data structure. The performer dynamically creates, mutates, and loads audio segments into playlists, which are exposed to the granular synthesis engine. The engine is controlled via global synthesis parameters including density, grain length, speed, pan, offset, and delay. This control model is situated against three prior studies involving live coding, music information retrieval, and personal information management, and demonstrated with a case study from a performance with code examples.</p>

<h3>Audience Evaluation of Networked Reverberation in Live Music Performance</h3>
<p>This paper reports an audience evaluation of the Auxtrument in performance with a live orchestra. The system functions as a distributed reverberation chamber, streaming real-time audio to remote sites and then back into the concert hall. Forty-seven post-concert responses were thematically analysed using an inductive coding scheme, and four themes were established: Imagination, Contextual Awareness, Transformative States, and Aesthetic Engagement. Overall, the Auxtrument was received as an effective and thought-provoking augmentation of concert hall space, though polarising in its balance between clarity, ambiguity, and conceptual framing.</p>

<h3>The Aural Cartographer's Dilemma</h3>
<p>This paper introduces the aural cartographer's dilemma: contemporary digital tools for spatial audio excel at mapping sound objects within coordinate systems but conflate coordinate-based representations of space with the embodied, relational experience of sound. It traces this inheritance from stereo panning through MPEG-I, demonstrating that successive technical paradigms instead reproduce the composer-score-conductor model through ever more sophisticated means. It argues this is not a technical limitation awaiting solution but a philosophical commitment requiring reconsideration.</p>

<h3>There and Back Again: Composing for Networked Reverberation Chamber and Live Orchestra</h3>
<p>This paper documents the deployment of a mobile, web-based, real-time distributed reverberation chamber named the Auxtrument, designed for a live concert setting. Rather than presenting a technical case study, it frames the project as an artistic and collaborative investigation into how networked audio systems can inform and transform musical composition and performance. In collaboration with a contemporary orchestra, two site-specific works were composed that use the inherent latency and uncertainty of WebRTC media streams over a mobile network as musical parameters rather than obstacles to overcome.</p>
<p><a href="https://austinfranklinmusic.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/There_and_Back_Again.pdf">Read more (PDF)</a></p>

<h3>PLISP: A PlayLISt Processing Language</h3>
<p>This paper introduces a text-based, domain-specific language for audio sample manipulation and playlist-based granular synthesis, embedded within the Cycling '74 Max v8ui environment using the mgraphics system, supporting real-time control and live coding. The interface enables command-line interaction with large-scale audio corpora organised on a 2D self-organising map. It employs LISP-inspired prefix syntax and nested expressions, enabling performers to navigate and manipulate audio material through gesture-based control.</p>
<p><a href="https://austinfranklinmusic.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/PLISP__a_PlayLISt_Processing_language.pdf">Read more (PDF)</a></p>

<h3>Development of a Web-Based Real-Time Distributed Reverberation Chamber for Live Concert Applications</h3>
<p>This paper documents the development and challenges encountered while creating a mobile, web-based, real-time distributed reverberation chamber for a live concert setting. WebRTC emerged as the most viable option after existing open-source and commercial solutions proved inadequate, offering peer-to-peer communication and firewall traversal. The paper concludes with reflections on decentralized audio routing and native app development, and calls for improved documentation and standardized debugging tools.</p>
<p><a href="https://austinfranklinmusic.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/JAES_V73_12_PG872hirez_Franklin.pdf">Read more (PDF)</a></p>

<h3>Pl&eacute;tude: New Paradigms for Notation with Game Engine-Based Scores</h3>
<p>This paper introduces Pl&eacute;tude, a game engine-based score merging music notation, gaming, and improvisation into a single artistic experience, where performers use acoustic instruments to control an on-screen avatar. Its four-note control system, inspired by early arcade games, and its visual design, rooted in modernist abstract art and visual music, fuse gaming and musical expression. The paper situates game engine-based scores within the broader history of music notation and discusses recent performances and player experiences.</p>
<p><a href="https://austinfranklinmusic.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Pletude__New_Paradigms_for_Notation_with_Game_Engine_Based_Scores.pdf">Read more (PDF)</a></p>
